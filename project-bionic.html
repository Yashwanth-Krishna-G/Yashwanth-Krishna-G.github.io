Variable Load Off-Loading Knee Exoskeleton

ANSYS MechanicalDual Lead ScrewBiomechanics Hardware Prototyping

Engineered a variable load off-loading knee exoskeleton prototype designed to assist mobility while preventing muscle atrophy[cite: 1]. The project focused heavily on an iterative design cycle, mechanical lifting mechanisms, and structural simulation before physical testing[cite: 1].

Iterative Design & ANSYS Simulation

The mechanical development spanned a comprehensive cycle of design and simulation, evolving from initial concepts to a finalized Version 5b which featured a redesigned lift mechanism[cite: 1].

ANSYS simulation was utilized throughout the design cycle to validate structural integrity and ensure precise alignment at the knee joint under varying load conditions[cite: 1].

Iterative design cycle and structural simulation from Version 1 to Version 5b[cite: 1].

Dual Lead Screw Lifting Mechanism

To achieve reliable mechanical assistance, a custom dual lead screw setup was engineered for the primary lifting mechanism[cite: 1].

The threads on the lead screw allow for variable displacement at the joint[cite: 1]. This mechanical configuration maintains strict alignment at the knee joint while providing the flexibility needed to precisely control the quantity of the offloading load[cite: 1].

Dual lead screw setup maintaining continuous knee joint alignment[cite: 1].

Variable Offloading & Biomechanical Impact

Unlike standard knee exoskeletons that apply a fixed support level, this system provides adjustable offloading to mitigate the muscle atrophy and osteoporosis typically associated with complete load removal[cite: 1].

The mechanical efficiency of the final prototype was successfully evaluated through displacement calculations; testing demonstrated that just 1mm of displacement at the knee joint effectively reduces the load on the cartilage by 16-20%[cite: 1].

Validation of variable displacement resulting in a 16-20% load reduction on cartilage[cite: 1].
