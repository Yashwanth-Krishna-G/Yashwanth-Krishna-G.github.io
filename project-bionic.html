Variable Load Off-Loading Knee Exoskeleton

ANSYS MechanicalSmart-Dial JointBiomechanics Hardware Prototyping

Engineered a variable load off-loading knee exoskeleton prototype designed to assist mobility while preventing muscle atrophy. The project focused heavily on an iterative design cycle, mechanical lifting mechanisms, and structural simulation before physical testing.

Iterative Design & ANSYS Simulation

The mechanical development spanned a comprehensive cycle of design and simulation, evolving from initial concepts to optimized iterations.

ANSYS simulation was utilized throughout the design cycle to validate structural integrity and ensure precise alignment at the knee joint under varying load conditions, minimizing deformation during maximum stress events.

Static structural deformation analysis simulated in ANSYS for Version 4.

Smart-Dial Lifting Mechanism

To achieve reliable mechanical assistance and load-bypassing, a purely mechanical, adjustable "smart-dial" joint was integrated with a custom lead screw setup for the primary lifting mechanism.

The dialed threads allow for variable displacement at the joint. This configuration maintains strict alignment at the knee joint while providing the physical flexibility needed to precisely control the quantity of the offloading load.

Mechanical "smart-dial" joint maintaining continuous knee joint alignment.

Variable Offloading & Physical Prototyping

Unlike standard knee exoskeletons that apply a fixed support level, this system provides adjustable offloading to mitigate the muscle atrophy and osteoporosis typically associated with complete load removal.

The mechanical efficiency of the final 3D-printed prototype was successfully evaluated by our team. Testing demonstrated that just 1mm of displacement at the knee joint effectively reduces the load on the cartilage by 16-20%.
