High-Performance Parallelization of an Unstructured Navier-Stokes-Fourier Solver

FortranOpenACCFinite Volume Method Ongoing M.Tech Thesis · IIT Guwahati

Developed as part of my M.Tech Thesis at IIT Guwahati: performance optimization and GPU parallelization of a fluid dynamics solver designed for high-speed compressible flow regimes. Targeting extreme atmospheric conditions through high-performance unstructured mesh frameworks.

Governing equations & formulation

The core algorithm employs a cell-centered Finite Volume Method (FVM) on unstructured meshes to solve the fully coupled Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations, handling complex thermal and viscous interactions.

\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathcal{V}} \mathbf{U} \, d\mathcal{V} + \oint_{\partial \mathcal{V}} \left( \mathbf{F}_c - \mathbf{F}_v \right) \cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0 \]

While primarily targeted at simulating the extreme aerodynamic and thermal conditions of atmospheric reentry vehicles, the robust unstructured framework ensures broad applicability across a wide spectrum of high-speed compressible flow geometries.


GPU acceleration & parallel computing

The primary objective is to implement advanced parallel computing techniques—specifically leveraging OpenACC directives within legacy Fortran architectures—to drastically reduce simulation turnaround times.

\[ T_{\text{speedup}} = \frac{T_{\text{serial CPU}}}{T_{\text{parallel GPU}}} \]

By refactoring compute-heavy kernels for hardware accelerators, the framework enables scalable, high-fidelity thermal and fluid analysis for complex, industrial-scale configurations.
